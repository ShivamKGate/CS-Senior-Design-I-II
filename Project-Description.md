# HepatoFusion Project Description

**Advisor:** Dr. Kalyani Patel (Kalyani.Patel@cchmc.org), Department of Pathology and Laboratory Medicine — Confirmed for 2-semester commitment until April 2027. Fit: Dr. Patel’s expertise in pediatric digital pathology and AI-assisted clinical workflows applies to HepatoFusion’s Silver/Gold whole-slide annotation pipeline and the pathology U-Net tumor-localization backbone that feeds multimodal risk stratification.  
**Additional advisors:** Dr. Sangaranjan Ranganathan, Dr. Surya Prasath, Christopher Woods. 

This Senior Design project: HepatoFusion, in collaboration with Cincinnati Children’s Hospital, develops a multimodal AI platform for pediatric liver cancer that both localizes tumor on digital pathology and estimates patient risk from complementary clinical data. An integrated data pipeline sits at the front of the system: whole-slide images (WSIs) and annotations move through a configurable Silver/Gold workflow, where provisional (Silver) labels are produced at scale and then expert-validated (Gold) for training and evaluation. This workflow currently covers 1,566+ Silver and 1,546+ Gold annotations across 248+ WSIs from 102+ patients, and it improved normal vs. aggressive tumor discrimination from 0.50 to 0.65 Dice and from 0.62 to 0.81 AUROC.

The pathology backbone is a high-resolution multiclass U-Net in PyTorch trained on this curated stream, achieving 0.96 mean slide Dice, 0.98 tumor Dice, and 0.99 AUROC for tumor localization. The same pipeline then fuses the pathology model with additional modalities: radiology imaging (CT, MRI), genomic and molecular profiles (primarily DNA), and clinical outcome records. The goal is a unified path from raw slides and aligned clinical inputs to tumor maps and a multimodal risk-stratification score, rather than treating each data source in isolation.

Deliverables include the end-to-end stack: data ingestion and Silver/Gold annotation, segmentation, multimodal fusion, and outcome prediction, plus evaluation against expert labels and clinical outcomes, reviewed with pathology stakeholders at Cincinnati Children’s. This project sits at the intersection of Data Engineering, Computer Vision, Image Processing, Multimodal Machine Learning, and Digital Pathology, connecting labeled data to patient-level risk estimates for outcome prediction.
